Why the pattern shows up even without real differences

Regression to the mean

A score is part skill and part luck on test day. Students placed in the top band are more likely to have had a lucky day, and most don't repeat it, so the group drifts down. The lowest band drifts up for the same reason.

Less precise at the extremes

The adaptive test has fewer items aimed at very high or very low ability, so individual scores at the ends carry more error. More error means a bigger drift back toward the middle the next year.

Floors and ceilings

The top band ends at the highest reportable score, so those students have little room up and lots of room down. The lowest band starts at the floor and can only climb.

What count does and doesn't do

Statewide, even the smallest band has thousands of students, so its median is stable. At a school, a band with a dozen students can swing by many points from a few noisy scores. Treat small local bands with care.

State benchmark by band

Use these as the yardstick for a school or district: take the local band's median change and subtract the state median for the same band. A positive difference means local students in that band grew more than similar students statewide. Mean minus median below zero means a tail of large drops; above zero, a tail of large gains.
